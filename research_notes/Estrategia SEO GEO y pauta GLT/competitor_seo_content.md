# Competitor SEO / GEO Content Strategies (benchmark for GLT Logistics, goglt.com)

Method note: research done 28 Oct 2026 using web search results and snippets only. A direct fetch of wearewarp.com/llms.txt failed because the host is blocked in this environment, so no competitor page was opened in full. Page-level claims below come from search-indexed titles, URLs and snippets. Most competitor figures (prices, savings %, counts) are self-published marketing and are marked as such. Search-tool summaries sometimes paraphrase, so a "[verify]" tag means the claim should be confirmed by opening the page.

## 1. Page types and scale (programmatic city/lane pages, comparisons, glossaries, tools, listicles, Spanish)

### Takeaway
Warp is the clear benchmark. It runs a large programmatic stack: lane pages, city LTL pages, city "services" pages, carrier rankings by city and state, drayage-by-city pages, glossary, /compare/, audience/industry pages, "best X" listicles, TMS partner pages and a Spanish /es section. Go Freight AI wins Miami queries with a different tactic: self-published "Top 10 [service] in Miami/Florida (2026)" directories on two domains, each ranking itself #1. ExFreight and BringGo Ship use "best X in [city/lane]" listicles plus long-form 2026 guides. C.H. Robinson relies on localized (es-mx/es-es/es-ar) white papers, guides and press releases.

### Inferences
- Warp duplicates the same city intent across several URL families: /ltl-shipping/, /local-ltl/, /city-services/, /ltl-carriers/ and /drayage/. Each targets a different modifier ("LTL shipping Miami", "LTL carriers Miami", "drayage Miami", "book freight Miami"), giving it multiple SERP chances for one city. The generic "LIVE LTL RATES" title on many pages and the inconsistent figures suggest the templates are machine-generated at scale.
- Go Freight's two (possibly three) domains and many service-by-city "Top 10 (2026)" lists are a deliberate GEO play. LLMs and AI Overviews readily quote "top 10 X in Miami" directories, and Go Freight appears #1 in each one it publishes.
- "Best [service] in [city/lane] [year]" listicles are the shared pattern across Go Freight, ExFreight, BringGo, Warp and CarrierSource. GLT currently competes against self-ranked lists in all of its core Miami and US-Mexico queries.
- Warp's /es section disclaims cross-border service and offers no Spanish support. That leaves an opening for GLT in Spanish-language cross-border plus Miami content, where Warp cannot credibly compete.
- On Warp's own /ltl-carriers/miami ranking, Warp is a "carrier" option. A Miami LTL/FTL provider like GLT could be benchmarked against it, or be absent from it.

## 2. GEO readiness signals (llms.txt, structured data, FAQ blocks, self-published rankings, MCP/API for agents)

### Takeaway
Warp is the most agent-ready competitor. Its public MCP server (warp-agent-mcp, live since 16 Apr 2026) quotes without an API key, has a hosted connector, and comes with per-client setup pages and SEO pages comparing it with rival MCPs. In Q3–Q4 2026, Freight Right, Flexport and FreightPOP followed with MCPs. ExFreight and FreightCenter publish llms(-full).txt files. Go Freight, ExFreight and BringGo lean on self-published rankings that LLMs readily cite.

### Cited Findings
- Warp MCP:
  - npm package warp-agent-mcp, published 16 Apr 2026. AI agents can quote, book and track LTL, FTL, cargo van, box truck and multi-stop shipments. It runs via npx in Claude Desktop, Claude Code and Cursor, and also as a hosted connector at mcp.wearewarp.com/api/mcp — [Glama listing](https://glama.ai/mcp/servers/warpfreight/warp-agent-mcp); [mcpservers.org](https://mcpservers.org/servers/warpfreight/warp-agent-mcp); [Socket npm](https://socket.dev/npm/package/warp-agent-mcp)

### Inferences
- MCP has become a 2026 positioning battleground among freight platforms: Warp in April, Flexport and FreightPOP around September, and Freight Right on 7 Oct. Warp also turned its MCP into SEO assets ("warp-vs-X-mcp" pages and per-client setup pages). For a 3PL of GLT's size, a full MCP is optional. A public, machine-readable quote/lane/service API, or at least an llms.txt and structured service data, would be the lower-cost equivalent.
- Self-published "Top 10 2026" directories are the dominant low-cost GEO tactic in Miami logistics. They are also a reputational risk: Go Freight markets them as "Verified" directories while ranking itself #1.

### Gaps
- Schema.org markup (FAQPage, Organization, Service, LocalBusiness) on any competitor could not be inspected without fetching pages.
- Whether Warp, Go Freight and BringGo publish llms.txt is unverified. The Warp fetch was blocked, and nothing about the others surfaced.
- Actual citation share in ChatGPT, Perplexity or AI Overviews for Miami 3PL / cross-border queries was not measured.
