# GEO visibility test: GLT Logistics (goglt.com), run 3, 28 Oct 2026

Method: each of the 16 buyer questions below went through the WebSearch tool once, in `extended` mode, with the wording exactly as given. WebSearch returns an AI-written answer that cites sources. For each one I recorded three things: whether GLT appears, which companies are named, and which domains are cited. I did not fetch any pages (the network is blocked), so every claim below comes from the WebSearch answer text and its cited link list. Results change from run to run, so this is one sample per question. Previous runs: v1 had GLT in 2/8, v2 in 3/8.

## Entity confusion and factual errors about GLT

### Takeaway
I found no factual errors about GLT that I could verify as wrong. The one "GLT" confusion risk (GL Group, Laredo) was handled correctly: the answer separated the two companies. There are small inconsistencies (years in business), and some claims depend on dated GLT content.

### Cited Findings
- **GL Group / GL Group Logistics (grupogl.com.mx)** is a customs broker in Laredo, TX with a Nuevo Laredo office. It was returned on the branded Q2, and the answer explicitly told the reader not to confuse it with GLT — [GL Group](https://grupogl.com.mx/)
- **Years in business are stated inconsistently:** "22+ years" in Q2 versus "more than 20 years" in Q3. Both come from goglt.com — [goglt.com](https://goglt.com/)
- **Possibly outdated framing:** Q2 cites a "2025-era" GLT article as saying GLT is "actively exploring how to promptly expand its market presence" in Mexico, and the answer inferred that its "Mexico footprint may be still growing." That conflicts with the "on-the-ground teams in Mexico" claim from the documentation guide — [Crossing Frontiers](https://goglt.com/crossing-frontiers-mexicos-influence-on-north-american-logistics/); [Documentation guide](https://goglt.com/mexico-cross-border-shipping-documentation/)
- The **"not a customs broker"** statement was repeated accurately from GLT's own page — [Documentation guide](https://goglt.com/mexico-cross-border-shipping-documentation/)
- A **near-name brand outside the GLT space:** "GoFreight" (gofreight.com, forwarder software, Q16) and "Go Freight / Go Freight AI" (go-freight.ai / go-freight.io, Miami 3PL, Q4/Q5/Q11/Q12) are different entities. Neither was confused with GLT.
- The Q1 answer attributed **"temperature-controlled" and "heavy haul"** services and a **June 2026** Magaya integration date to GLT/Magaya sources. These match the cited pages as summarized, but I could not check them against the live pages — [Magaya press release](https://www.magaya.com/magaya-and-glt-logistics-launch-integration-to-streamline-inland-transportation-workflows/)

### Inferences
- The old "Crossing Frontiers" post weakens GLT's Mexico positioning in AI answers. Updating or dating it would remove the "still exploring Mexico" signal.
- Using one consistent years-in-business figure across goglt.com would remove the 20 versus 22 inconsistency.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm the actual founding year or whether the Magaya integration date is exactly June 2026 without fetching pages.
- I saw no confusion with other "GLT"-named firms (e.g., other GLT Logistics or GLT Transport entities) in this run. That is limited to these 16 queries.
